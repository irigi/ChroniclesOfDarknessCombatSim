# CoD core — World, lexicon, storytelling, the God-Machine (Intro, Ch. 3, Ch. 5)

## The setting [pp. 17–20]
- The Chronicles of Darkness = our world "slightly wrong"; horror from the uncanny and from people. Protagonists
  are mortals who glimpse the supernatural; other game lines play the monsters (Vampire, Werewolf, Mage,
  Promethean, Changeling, Hunter, Geist, Mummy, Demon, Beast) [p. 20].
- Everyone has glimpsed and forgotten something (a creation breaking point) [p. 30].
- Five units of dramatic time: turn (3 s), scene, chapter (= one session), story, chronicle [p. 73].

## Lexicon highlights [pp. 20–21]
- **Aggravated** = grievous/supernatural wound (vampires from fire, werewolves from silver), heals 1/week;
  **bashing** heals 1/15 min; lethal 1/2 days.
- **Chance roll**: pool ≤ 0 → one die, success only on 10, 1 = dramatic failure.
- **Close combat** uses Strength; **ranged** uses Dexterity; Defense normally not vs firearms.
- **Resistance**: usually reflexive (Resolve/Composure/Stamina subtract or contest).
- **Scene** typically ends when characters leave the location or when combat ends.
- Specialty +1 die, any number per Skill.
- 8-again/9-again/10-again: rerolled dice keep exploding.

## Storyteller guidance (Ch. 3 "Black Threads", pp. 106–115) — no new mechanics
- Shared Storyteller responsibilities; cheerlead for PCs; give ST characters Aspirations.
- Scene framing ("Spines"): pitch the scene, central conflict, set dressing, in medias res, connective tissue.
- **Pulled Muscles**: failure fuels Beats/Conditions; ST may offer **success at a cost** or "pick your poison"
  choices instead of dead-end failures; move unused material ("Schrödinger's character"); tie hooks to Aspirations.
- **Story kits** (Exquisite Corpse): situation paragraph + why-care bullets + 3 principal ST characters (full
  stats, 3 Aspirations) + 2–3 general characters (dice pools only, 2 Aspirations).
- "Prick the skin": aim for disquiet, not gore.
- **Missing Persons** chronicle starter: build the setting map around a disappearance — the person, crime
  scenes (each player adds a location), implication (PC ties), community rumors, authorities, people of interest.

## Ephemeral world (summary; rules in 06) [pp. 122–124]
- **Twilight**: the invisible, intangible state of ghosts, spirits, angels.
- **Ghosts**: echoes of the dead held by **Anchors**; fed by remembrance; anchorless ghosts fall into the
  **Underworld** (Great Below, Tartarus, Dominions) via **Avernian gates** (in Death-Resonant places; each has a key).
- **Spirits**: reflections of everything but humans, living in the **Shadow** behind the **Gauntlet** (thicker
  where many people are; Loci are thin spots); they eat Essence and each other; refugees in our world need
  Resonant hosts. Speak a Sumerian-like tongue.
- **Angels**: instruments of the **God-Machine**, made/brought via **Infrastructure**; single-minded missions.
- Other games reuse these rules for their own entities (sidebar p. 124). Honorary Rank: Sin-Eaters vs ghosts,
  werewolf Renown vs spirits (p. 128).
- **Supernatural Tolerance** (sidebar p. 125): Rank / Blood Potency / Primal Urge / Gnosis etc. are
  interchangeable for resisting powers. Example: vampire's power vs angel → penalised by angel's Resistance + Rank.

## The God-Machine (Ch. 5, pp. 162–237)
- Prologue myth (Marco Singe, "Pain Prophet"): First Children (angels/Ancients), Second (demons), Third (mankind);
  sky-city of the Ancients; mankind's revolt; eight surviving Furies (Silence, Death, Torment, Fear, Strife…);
  numbers stations, Apollo/"Packet Theta" — **unreliable in-world testimony** [pp. 162–169].
- **What it is: deliberately undefined** [p. 171]. A literal machine spanning the Earth with tendrils elsewhere;
  not omnipresent, omnipotent or omniscient; obeys physics it understands far better; slow, methodical,
  indifferent (not sadistic) to humans; rarely retries a thwarted gambit in the same place [pp. 171–173].
- **Infrastructure** types [p. 173]: Concealment (fronts, illusions — seen through by *Unseen Sense: God-Machine*;
  once you see a gear you can always see them), Defense (cultists, monsters, servitors), Logistical (moving people
  and materials), Elimination/"scrubbing", Command & Control (never found).
- **Occult matrix** → **Output** (usually an angel); precise timing; failure usually does nothing. Outputs feed
  further Infrastructure [p. 174]. Piggybacks on mortal infrastructure (post office, military bases) — killing
  unwitting pawns has Integrity and legal consequences [p. 174 sidebar].
- **Linchpins**: weak points (suspicious front, unfaithful servant, lazy scrubbers, mortal politics,
  communications hub, the man at the top, breeding project, the occupation, seraphim, "if you can't beat them").
  Striking one should yield insight or local victory; the God-Machine moves elsewhere [pp. 175–177].
- **Optional rule: Fate** [p. 177]: a special Aspiration naming the character's doom ("Murdered", "Betrayed",
  "Imprisoned", "Transformed" — the *what*, not how/when). Fulfilled → **a full Experience**. When an action leads
  toward the Fate, **spending WP gives only +2 dice, but the WP is refunded if the action fails**. Ghosts have Fate
  "Forgotten"; spirits get Fates like "Bound", "Consumed", "Starved"; angels none. Numen Fate Sense reads it.
  **Destiny Merit** is referenced (bearers always have Infrastructure; can't contest Fate Sense) but **not
  defined in this book** (from *God-Machine Chronicle*).
- **Tiers** [pp. 180–186]: Local (neighborhood, unwitting pawns), Regional (city/state, cults, larger
  Infrastructure), Global (world-spanning schemes, Atlantis/Bermuda-type lore, "Black Tide", Cult of the Wheel),
  Cosmic (other worlds and times). Chronicles can slide between tiers.
- **Assembly**: building chronicles from Tales (Spinning Cogs, Into the Machine, Progression) [pp. 186–188].
- **Chronicle tracks** [pp. 188–193]: Protect and Serve (cops/first responders), Rage Against the God-Machine
  (active resistance), Space & Time (temporal tales), Wanderers (travelers).
- **Tales of the God-Machine** [pp. 196–237]; each Tale has Infrastructure, Interchangeable Parts, Blueprints,
  Linchpins, Methods (suggested Skills/Merits), Escalation:
  - Local: **The 300 Block** (boiler room sacrifices; Architect) p. 197 · **Do-Over** (diner time-room, lose WP
    per trip; Jenn) p. 199 · **Sister City** (Seattle/Old Seattle; Polis Men) p. 201 · **The Squares of the City**
    (buildings move at night on rails; Officer Strait) p. 203 · **Wellington School for Gifted Children**
    (God-Machine blind spot; Emilio Rubio, Sarah Andrews) p. 205.
  - Regional: **Ghost Machine** (ghosts bound into machines; Black Nathaniel) p. 207 · **The Key** (Wesley Cote;
    Grief) p. 208 · **Missing Persons** (factory/processing; Crossover spots) p. 210 · **The Moon Window** p. 212 ·
    **Wake the Dead** (Lucien McJack, Mirror Vulture) p. 214.
  - Global: **A Glimpse of Mesmerizing Complexity** p. 216 · **The Invisible Citadel** (Dalga, clockworks) p. 219 ·
    **Operation: Bell Jar** (West Nautical 36) p. 221 · **Proposition 279** (Mr. Nose) p. 223 · **The Scarlet
    Plague** (Serotonin) p. 225.
  - Cosmic: **The Hatching** (Statue of Liberty; Kalka) p. 226 · **A Journey Into Time** (Dr. Girard) p. 228 ·
